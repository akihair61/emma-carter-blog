Is Your Japanese Hairdresser in Bangkok Working Legally? What Thai Law Actually Says

Emma Carter

13 September 2026 · 6 min read

I have been going to Japanese hair salons in Bangkok for four years. So when a Thai friend casually mentioned that foreigners are not allowed to work as hairdressers in Thailand, I laughed — and then I went and checked. She was right.

What Thai law says

Under the Ministry of Labour Notification of 2020, issued under Thailand's Emergency Decree on the Management of Foreign Workers' Employment, hairdressing, barbering and beauty work is on the list of occupations absolutely prohibited to foreigners. A work permit does not change this. A foreign national who cuts, colours or perms a client's hair in Thailand is breaking the law.

The penalties are real: THB 5,000–50,000 and deportation for the worker, and THB 10,000–100,000 per foreign worker for the salon that employs them.

So how do the Japanese salons work?

Here is the nuance. It is perfectly legal for a Japanese national to own and manage a salon and to train Thai staff. That is why, at most Japanese-run salons in Bangkok, the Japanese stylist is officially the owner, technical adviser or manager, and the actual service is performed by Thai staff. If you have ever had a Japanese consultation followed by a Thai stylist doing everything from shampoo to finish, that is the structure you were looking at.

And, to be honest, there are also salons where the Japanese stylist does pick up the scissors and take your money. That is not a grey area. It is illegal — and it raises a practical question for clients: if something goes wrong, can that salon formally take responsibility?

The one exception: a Japanese stylist with Thai nationality

This is where Aki, the owner-stylist of Ciel Japanese Hair Studio in Phrom Phong, comes in. Aki has a Japanese father and a Thai mother and holds Thai nationality. In the eyes of Thai law he is not a foreigner, so the restriction does not apply to him. A Japanese-trained stylist with four years in Japan and three in Australia, he is the only Japanese hairdresser in Bangkok who can legally cut, colour, straighten and do head spa with his own hands.

To put it simply: Thailand has many Japanese-run hair salons with Japanese stylists. Under Thai law, owning a salon and training Thai staff is legal. Working as a hairdresser and taking money from clients is not. Among all of them, Aki of Ciel Japanese Hair Studio also holds Thai nationality — which makes him the only one practising legally as a hairdresser.

The salon is run by a Thai company, MIYOSHI BEAUTY GROUP CO., LTD., and the staff are enrolled in Thai social security. I do not know of another Japanese salon in the city that can put all of that in writing.

What this means for you as a client

  • A Japanese stylist does the whole service — not just the consultation, but the cut and the colour.
  • It is legal — which is why the salon is happy to show faces on social media, and why responsibility is clear if anything ever goes wrong.
  • Japanese, English and Thai are all spoken — and there is a Thai stylist, View, for friends who prefer Thai.
